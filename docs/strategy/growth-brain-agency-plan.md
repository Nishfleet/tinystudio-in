# Growth Brain Agency Plan

## Mission

Make money quickly by selling a useful, productized marketing ops service that can be delivered before software exists.

## Positioning

TinyStudio is not a generic AI agency. It is a Growth Brain operator.

The client does not buy "AI". They buy human-reviewed **The Website Correction** on one highest-leverage page: a prioritized fault map, rewrite or redesign, one implementation pass or dev-ready handoff, search-trust basics, before/after proof, a Loom, a measurement plan, one revision, and 14-day implementation tracking.

## Why This Can Work Now

- Businesses already have too much disconnected context.
- AI output is weak when it has no business memory.
- Small teams need faster marketing execution, not another strategy deck.
- Nish can deliver the first version manually with Codex before building software.

## The Flywheel

1. Context: collect the client's website, reviews, ads, emails, analytics, competitors, and founder notes.
2. Brain: organize the context into a reusable client brain.
3. Agents: run repeatable workflows against the brain.
4. Approval: human checks every claim and recommendation.
5. Delivery: hand over fixes, drafts, and next actions.
6. Measurement: review what happened.
7. Learning: update the brain and repeat.

## First Niche

Start with founder-led Managed IT, MSP, and cybersecurity companies that already have a live site, high-value offer, and real context.

Do not pick pre-revenue startups first. The sprint needs raw material: reviews, pages, ads, emails, analytics, search intent, and competitors.

## Delivery Stack

- Client brain template
- Seven agent workflows
- Loom walkthrough
- Delivery template
- Weekly report template
- Mobbin/reference workflow for page direction when needed

## Agency Scale Model

The sprint gets the first cash. The operating model creates the larger business.

TinyStudio should use agents to systemize one service line, keep a human approval layer over it, then expand into adjacent marketing functions. The long-term retainer should feel like an outsourced growth desk: one relationship, one weekly loop, and one place where the client's marketing context keeps getting smarter.

Do not jump straight to a full-service menu. Start with the sharpest wedge, prove it, systemize it, then add the next lane.

## SEO Architecture Wedge

For founder-led Managed IT, MSP, and cybersecurity clients, the fastest useful audit is often not "write more content". It is:

- focus the homepage on the most valuable buying intent
- create or clarify dedicated service pages
- rebuild heading hierarchy
- rewrite sections as buying pages, not blog posts
- add localized or niche-specific trust sections
- add FAQ coverage for branded and unbranded questions
- capture phrases customers and AI/search surfaces already associate with the category

This becomes a strong Loom audit because the prospect can see the fault immediately.

## Sales Math

To close the first 3 clients:

- Identify 200 businesses.
- Research 50.
- Record 25 personalized Loom audits.
- Send 25 direct messages or emails.
- Book 8-10 calls.
- Close 3 founder-priced sprints.

## Non-Goals

- Do not build SaaS first.
- Do not run paid ads for TinyStudio first.
- Do not make a large marketing website first.
- Do not promise specific revenue results.
- Do not hire before the first delivery loop is proven.
