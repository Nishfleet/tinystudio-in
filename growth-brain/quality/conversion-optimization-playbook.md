# Conversion Optimization Playbook

Use this on every landing page, sales page, lead form, product page, and client-facing page recommendation.

This is an internal heuristic layer, not public proof. Do not quote outside benchmark percentages, creator claims, or screenshots as client-facing evidence. If a recommendation needs proof, put it in the claim-proof ledger.

## Rule

Every page audit must return:

- pass/fail score for the critical checks
- top 5 failures ranked by likely buyer impact
- one exact fix per failure
- approval-needed claims
- one measurement plan

Do not ship a new page or final page rewrite that fails the first five critical checks.

## Direct Response Copy Layer

Write like a smart friend who figured something out and is trying to help.

Use this sequence when rewriting a section:

1. Headline: make the big promise clear.
2. Problem: name the costly or painful current state.
3. Agitate: make the cost vivid without exaggerating.
4. Credibility: show why the advice is worth hearing.
5. Solution: show the transformation or path.
6. Proof: use specific, approved evidence.
7. CTA: tell the buyer the beneficial next step.

Before final copy, run the So-What chain:

```text
Feature -> functional result -> financial or operational result -> emotional result
```

Write from the emotional result, then support it with the functional and financial logic.

Style rules:

- Short. Then breathe. Land it.
- Replace vague claims with precise facts.
- If proof is not available, mark the claim for approval or remove it.

## Positioning Angle Layer

Pick the angle before writing copy.

### Match Market Stage

| Market Stage | Default Angle |
|---|---|
| New | Simple promise: "Now you can..." |
| Growing | Bigger claim with specific time, scope, or pain. |
| Crowded | Show the mechanism that makes the offer different. |
| Jaded | Prove it with data, process, or visible proof. |
| Mature | Sell identity, status, certainty, or a specific fit. |

### Find The Mechanism

Ask:

- What do competitors skip?
- What does this client know or do that the market does not see?
- Can we name the method without inventing a fake framework?

### Pick One Angle

- Contrarian: what common belief is wrong?
- Transformation: from painful state to desired state.
- Enemy: what bad habit, tool, or assumption are we replacing?
- Speed: what can happen faster without cutting quality?
- Specificity: who is this exactly for, and what exact thing do they get?

### Test It

The angle must be:

- specific, not vague
- differentiated, not a commodity promise
- believable with the proof available

## Email Sequence Layer

Use this for warm nurture, launch, abandoned action, or post-lead sequences. Do not use it to spam cold non-responders.

| Phase | Timing | Job | Example Angle |
|---|---|---|---|
| Activate | Days 0-3 | Give value and get a quick win. | Deliver the asset, invite a two-minute action, ask whether they tried it. |
| Reveal | Days 3-7 | Explain why the quick win worked. | Show the method, system, and proof behind the result. |
| Convert | Days 8-14 | Make the offer and handle objections. | Pitch with options, show the math, answer the main objection, show the anti-vision, ask non-buyers why they did not buy. |

Principles:

- The first 48 hours are for activation, not hard selling.
- Show value before explaining value.
- Use anti-vision carefully: name the cost of doing nothing without fearmongering.
- Ask non-buyers for feedback so the brain learns.
- Respect opt-out, sender setup, and channel rules.

## Distribution Moat Layer

Use this when a client needs more reach, not just a better page.

| Principle | Workflow Translation |
|---|---|
| Distribution beats sameness | Product and page quality matter, but reach and memory compound. |
| Start from zero to one | Find the first channel that can create real replies before scaling. |
| True fans compound | Build trust with a small reachable audience before chasing broad attention. |
| Velocity beats perfection | Ship useful pieces consistently, then let feedback refine them. |
| Write the playbook | Publish the useful method instead of copying generic category content. |
| Referral physics | Make sharing feel helpful to the sender, not like a favor to the brand. |
| Platform arbitrage | Find places where the audience is active and supply is weak. |
| Micro-value bridges | Turn one strong idea into small useful posts, clips, replies, and emails. |
| Audience ownership | Move rented attention toward email, community, demos, calls, or direct relationships. |
| Audience-talent stack | Good content attracts collaborators, partners, and proof sources. |
| Feedback loop speed | Smaller audiences can give faster qualitative feedback than dashboards. |
| Response-rate edge | Replying quickly and thoughtfully is a distribution advantage. |
| Content multiplication | One research insight should become multiple channel-native assets. |
| Value-stack playbook | Layer idea, proof, useful example, and next action. |
| First-principles distribution | Understand why people share, search, buy, or refer in this market. |
| Cross-pollination | Move useful ideas across channels without pasting the same asset everywhere. |
| Distribution equity | Treat audience trust as an asset that grows with useful repetition. |
| Hook engineering | Open with the real tension, then deliver value quickly. |
| Community loops | Give people a reason to return, respond, and bring others. |
| Distribution attractant | Good reach attracts partners, talent, backlinks, and better opportunities. |

## Search Trust Layer

Use this after the conversion fix is clear. Conversion fixes come first; search trust comes second.

On-site search trust checks:

- page title and meta description
- one clear H1 and logical H2/H3 structure
- internal links to the next best service, proof, FAQ, pricing, booking, or contact page
- service-page structure with who it is for, problem, solution, proof, objections, FAQ, and CTA
- FAQ blocks based on real buyer questions
- schema only when the visible page facts support it
- sitemap, canonical, robots, crawl, and index basics
- local or service-area relevance when the business serves a geography
- non-commodity content, examples, proof, or methodology that a generic AI answer could not invent

Off-site SEO is only trust/distribution:

- real citations and reputable directories
- Google Business Profile improvements
- partner, vendor, marketplace, or association listings
- review and testimonial collection
- founder, team, client, or project proof assets
- useful pages people may naturally cite
- real PR, podcast, community, or local mentions

Never recommend buying backlinks, link schemes, fake citations, fake reviews, spam directory blasts, doorway pages, hidden text, keyword stuffing, or ranking guarantees.

## Paid Ads Ops Layer

Only use this after a client has real campaign data or a paid ads budget.

Weekly loop:

1. Pull the last 7 days of spend, clicks, CPA/CAC, creative fatigue, audience, and landing-page data.
2. Identify winning audiences, dying creatives, and message-match breaks.
3. Pull fresh inspiration from approved competitor/reference sources.
4. Generate new hooks and direct-response copy variants from the winning audience insight.
5. Human-review claims, compliance, offer, and proof.
6. Launch small controlled tests and pause clear losers.
7. Write a morning brief with what changed, what was paused, and what to watch.

Do not automate launch or pause actions until client permissions, spend limits, and rollback rules are explicit.

## AI/Search No-Hack Layer

Use this whenever a page, content plan, or site-architecture recommendation touches AEO, GEO, AI search, or organic discovery.

Good AI/search visibility still starts with good SEO: crawlable pages, clear page jobs, useful answer sections, true proof, strong internal links, and content that helps a human buyer.

Reject these shortcuts:

- LLMs.txt files, AI.txt files, special markdown, or AI-only pages as a magic visibility lever.
- Chunking pages into tiny pieces only for AI systems.
- Rewriting content only to chase AI phrasing instead of improving buyer clarity.
- Seeking fake mentions, spammy forum references, or inauthentic third-party citations.
- Treating structured data as required for generative AI visibility.
- Buying backlinks, using link schemes, or creating off-site assets only to manipulate rankings.

Use structured data when it fits the normal SEO strategy and the on-page facts support it. If the page cannot answer the buyer's question clearly for a human, fix that first.
