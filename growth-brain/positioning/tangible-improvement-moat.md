# Tangible Improvement Moat

TinyStudio is a new agency with no earned market trust yet. The wedge is not a vague claim that we are better at marketing. The wedge is that every sprint shows the improvement delta clearly enough that the buyer can inspect it without trusting us first.

## Promise

Show the client what changed, why it matters, what proof backs it, and what gets measured next.

## Why This Can Become The Moat

Most agency work asks the client to trust taste, strategy, activity, or vague "growth" claims. TinyStudio should earn trust by showing a weekly proof delta the client can inspect:

- the fault before
- the improvement after
- the source that proves it
- the business value in plain English
- the measurement contract: signal, source, owner, next check, baseline/current state, and decision rule

The durable advantage is not one dashboard. It is the repeated habit of shipping, proving, explaining, and re-measuring improvements faster than a traditional agency can package a recommendation.

The real retention proof is customer-perceived value. Each week the client should confirm that they saw the delta, understood why it matters, approved the next action, and gave a continue/retain signal.

## Proof Unit

Every meaningful delivery needs one tangible improvement row:

| Field | Required Standard |
|---|---|
| Before | The confusing, leaking, or unmeasured previous state. |
| After | The improved page, copy, architecture, offer, workflow, or measurement state. |
| Proof source | Screenshot, page source, test result, analytics, client fact, or shipped file. |
| Client-visible value | The plain-English business value the client can understand without trusting us. |
| Next measurement | The next signal that will prove whether the improvement mattered. |
| Measurement contract | The signal, source, owner, next check, baseline/current state, and Decision rule. |

## Operating Rule

Do not sell "marketing strategy" as the product. Sell a visible improvement loop:

1. Find one high-value fault.
2. Ship or hand off one exact improvement.
3. Show the before and after.
4. State the evidence.
5. Pick the next measurement.
6. Name the owner, source, next check, baseline/current state, and decision rule.
7. Ask what felt valuable and what felt unclear.
8. Confirm the client saw the value and agreed to the next action.
9. Repeat weekly.

## What This Is Not

- Not a guarantee of revenue.
- Not proof that competitors lack this workflow.
- Not a replacement for external market proof, replies, closes, or retained paid clients.
- Not a dashboard-only moat. Dashboards are common. The moat is the proof discipline, fast shipping, and client-visible learning loop.

## Moat Test

If a client cannot answer "what changed this week?" and "why did that matter?" in under 30 seconds, the workflow failed.

If the client cannot also answer "what should we do next?" and "is this worth continuing?", retention risk is still open.
